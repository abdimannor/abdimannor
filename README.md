## Academic Focus

### Institutional Analysis

How organisations and public authorities construct legitimacy, accountability, and social order. My interest lies in the gap between formal rules and actual practice — how institutions are interpreted, contested, and reproduced in day-to-day interactions. This includes both public administration and civil society organisations, and how each negotiates authority and trust over time.

### Welfare State & Governance

Public policy, state capacity, and the distribution of social outcomes. Particular attention to integration policy and labour market access — who the welfare state reaches, who it doesn't, and what institutional conditions explain the difference. The welfare state as both a set of formal rules and a site of continuous political negotiation.

### Social Research Methodology

Qualitative and quantitative approaches to studying social phenomena. How to ask the right question before choosing a method, and how to combine interpretive depth with structural analysis without losing rigour. Understanding what different methods can and can't tell you, and why that distinction matters for the conclusions you draw.

### Risk, Democracy & Change

How information asymmetry, uncertainty, and political transformation affect institutions and collective action. The conditions under which democratic systems adapt — or fail to — when faced with rapid change. Who bears the risk when institutions are slow to respond, and how that risk is distributed across social groups.

## Approach

Sociology and code share more than they might appear to. Both are fundamentally about systems — how they're structured, how they produce outcomes, and where the leverage points are. Sociology gives me frameworks for asking why things work the way they do. Code gives me a way to build things from scratch and understand the constraints that go into any system that actually runs.

That overlap shapes how I work on both sides: analytically rigorous in the academic work, structurally minded in the technical work. The goal is to develop both in parallel rather than treating them as separate tracks.
